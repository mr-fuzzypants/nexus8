# Part A — SR&ED Narrative

## A1. Project objective

Extend nexus8's mask annotation and generative editing capabilities — currently proven on still images (see `SRED_INPAINT_EXPERIMENT.md`) — to video, by developing three coupled capabilities within the existing frame-accurate video annotator:

1. **Mask tracks via interactive propagation**: the artist scribbles or clicks on a single frame; a video segmentation model (SAM 2) propagates the mask bidirectionally across the clip, producing a per-frame *mask track*. The artist corrects the mask on any frame where it drifts, and propagation re-runs from the correction — an interactive refinement loop analogous to the still-image live inpaint loop.

2. **Temporally consistent object removal**: a propagated mask track drives a video inpainting model that removes the masked object across all frames with temporal consistency — the video analog of the still-image `remove` operation.

3. **Keyframe generative edit + propagation**: a generative edit performed on one keyframe with the existing `nexus8-inpaint` pipeline (scribble-infill, prompted replace) is propagated to neighbouring frames via optical flow / patch-based transfer constrained by the mask track — avoiding per-frame diffusion, which is known to produce temporal flicker.

No commercial annotation or asset-management tool couples frame-accurate annotation, interactive mask propagation, and generative video operations in one editing surface with full provenance. Commercial video removal tools (Runway inpainting, Adobe Firefly video) are round-trip black boxes with no annotation integration, no correction loop, and no versioned provenance of what was removed and how.

## A2. Technological uncertainties

### U1 — Interactive propagation quality and the correction-loop protocol

SAM 2 propagates masks from prompt frames, but published evaluations use benchmark clips. It is unknown, on real production content (occlusions, motion blur, similar-textured backgrounds, shots longer than SAM 2's memory-bank horizon): (a) how far a single-frame prompt propagates before drift makes the track unusable; (b) whether the correction loop (artist fixes frame *k*, propagation re-runs) converges in a small number of corrections or oscillates (a correction at frame *k* degrading previously-correct frames); (c) what the interaction protocol should be — re-propagate the whole clip from all accumulated prompts, or only the span between the corrected frame and the next user-verified frame; and (d) whether the job latency (seconds to a minute per clip) is compatible with an interactive correction loop at all, or whether the loop must be restructured around span-limited incremental re-propagation to keep per-correction latency low.

### U2 — Representing mask tracks in the entity/version data model

The platform's annotation model is per-frame (image2d annotations bound to a frame via mediaBinding, floor-based frame math). A mask track is a new kind of object: per-frame geometry over a frame range, produced partly by the artist (prompt/correction frames) and partly by a model (propagated frames), with provenance distinguishing the two. It is unknown: (a) whether tracks should be stored as vector geometry per frame (consistent with the v1 vector-annotation decision, but propagation models emit rasters — vectorization cost and fidelity unknown), as rasterized per-frame mask PNGs (consistent with the still-image mask pipeline but ~frame-count × mask storage), or as a hybrid (vector on prompt frames, raster on propagated frames); (b) whether the always-new-version convention scales when a single correction invalidates hundreds of downstream propagated frames — is a corrected track a new version of the whole track, or a patch over a frame span; and (c) how the two-axis versioning scheme (`LAYER_RENDER_SCHEMA.md`, INP-F13) extends to per-frame-range outputs.

### U3 — Job-scale GPU orchestration and frame transport

The still-image loop is interactive-latency (INP-F4: 4.6 s warm). Video propagation and removal are batch jobs (seconds to minutes) with much larger payloads. It is unknown: (a) how to ship frames to Modal efficiently — the candidate models consume frame sequences, not video containers, so the ingest path (ffprobe-based) must be extended to extract and stage frame sequences server-side, with unknown storage/latency cost per clip; (b) whether the dispatch/poll protocol from the still pipeline extends to multi-minute jobs or requires a proper async job model with progress reporting and cancellation; and (c) what the cost envelope per removal job is on A100-class hardware (VOID needs 40 GB+ VRAM; Wan-VACE-14B similar; Wan-VACE-1.3B runs at 480p on ~8–24 GB), and whether a resolution/quality ladder (preview at 480p on cheap GPUs, final at full resolution on A100) is necessary to keep iteration affordable.

### U4 — Temporal consistency and hallucination control in generative removal

Flow-completion models (ProPainter class) borrow real pixels from other frames and are temporally stable but cannot synthesize content that is never visible; diffusion-based removers can synthesize disoccluded content but risk hallucinating objects (the video analog of INP-F3's salience problem) and temporal flicker. It is unknown, for the license-viable candidates (see F1–F6 below): (a) whether Wan-VACE — a general text-guided masked editor, not a dedicated remover — can be reliably steered to *remove* (fill with background) rather than replace, via prompt/negative-prompt strategy alone, and whether a prompt model analogous to the still-image findings (INP-F8) exists for video; (b) whether VOID's purpose-built removal (including removal of the object's physical effects — shadows, reflections, interactions) holds up on content outside its demo distribution, and how its 85-frame window + flow-warped-noise second pass behaves on shots longer than the window; and (c) where the quality boundary lies between cheap flow-completion (MIT-licensed FGT) and diffusion-based removal — i.e., for what shot classes (static background, small occlusion) the classical model suffices.

### U5 — Keyframe edit propagation without per-frame diffusion

Running the still-image diffusion pipeline per frame is known to flicker (no temporal conditioning). The proposed alternative — edit one keyframe, propagate the edited region via optical flow (RAFT) or patch-based transfer constrained to the mask track — has no established recipe. It is unknown: (a) how quickly propagation drift and disocclusion degrade the edit (frames-per-keyframe before a re-key is needed, as a function of motion magnitude); (b) how to handle disoccluded regions within the propagated edit — inpaint them per-frame with the LCM pipeline (fast mode, INP-F6) and blend, or leave them to a video model; and (c) whether the hybrid (flow-propagated pixels + LCM fill for disocclusions) is temporally stable enough to be usable, or whether generative keyframe edits on video require a true video diffusion model (Wan-VACE with a reference-frame conditioning path) to be viable at all.

### U6 — Directing artist review over hundreds of propagated frames

A 10 s / 24 fps clip yields 240 propagated mask frames; frame-by-frame review would erase the productivity gain of propagation. The proposed mitigation is to surface SAM 2's per-frame confidence on the layer's timeline lane as low-confidence spans, directing the artist to scrub only the flagged stretches. It is unknown: (a) whether SAM 2's confidence scores actually *predict* the frames a human would correct — published confidence measures are calibration artifacts of the model, and their correlation with perceptual mask failure (edge slop, partial occlusion leakage) on real content is undocumented; (b) if raw confidence is a weak predictor, whether cheap derived signals do better (frame-to-frame mask-area discontinuity, IoU drop between consecutive frames, distance from nearest prompt frame); and (c) what the false-negative tolerance is — a "review here" indicator that misses real failures is worse than no indicator, because it licenses the artist to skip review elsewhere.

### U7 — Commercially viable model selection under heterogeneous licensing

The strongest published models in this space carry non-commercial licenses (ProPainter: S-Lab 1.0; E2FGVI: CC BY-NC-SA; MiniMax-Remover: CC BY-NC weights; CoTracker3: CC BY-NC). It was unknown at project start which models across the full pipeline (segmentation/propagation, point tracking, optical flow, removal/inpainting) are usable in a commercial product, whether permissive-license alternatives are competitive in quality, and what license conditions attach to the diffusion base models (community licenses with MAU caps, revenue thresholds, territorial exclusions). **This uncertainty was substantially resolved by the August 1 2026 landscape survey — see findings F1–F6.**

## A3. Hypotheses

- **H1 (Single-prompt propagation horizon):** A single-frame SAM 2 prompt produces a usable mask track for typical shots (≤10 s, moderate motion), with drift concentrated at occlusion events; the correction loop converges in ≤3 corrections per shot for production-usable tracks.
- **H2 (Hybrid track representation):** Mask tracks are best stored as rasterized per-frame masks (reusing the still-image mask asset pipeline and content-addressed store) with vector geometry retained only on prompt/correction frames; the track is one asset whose versions follow the two-axis scheme — each propagation run a `version_number`, with provenance (prompt frames, model, parameters) in `Version.data`, extending INP-F13 without new tables.
- **H3 (Job protocol):** The still pipeline's dispatch/poll protocol extends to video jobs with the addition of coarse progress (frames processed / total) in the status payload and a cancellation endpoint; a full task-queue framework is not required at single-user experimental scale.
- **H4 (Removal quality ladder):** Wan-VACE-1.3B at 480p serves as the fast/preview removal tier and validates the pipeline end-to-end; VOID (A100-40GB) is the quality tier for final removal, with its quadmask input populated directly from SAM 2 mask tracks (object region; interaction region optional/empty initially). Flow-completion (FGT) suffices for static-background shots and is worth keeping as a cheap tier.
- **H5 (Prompt strategy transfers):** The still-image prompt findings (INP-F8: describe objects, recruit the prior, mask generously) transfer to Wan-VACE removal prompting — "empty street, asphalt" style background description outperforms instruction-style prompts, and generous masks reduce edge artifacts.
- **H6 (Keyframe propagation envelope):** Flow-propagated keyframe edits remain acceptable for spans of low-to-moderate motion (order 10–30 frames per keyframe) with LCM-filled disocclusions; fast motion or rotation requires re-keying frequently enough that the approach degenerates to per-frame editing, defining the boundary where a video diffusion model becomes necessary.
- **H7 (Confidence-directed review):** SAM 2 per-frame confidence, combined with cheap derived signals (frame-to-frame mask-area discontinuity, consecutive-frame IoU drop), flags the frames a human would correct with high enough recall that timeline-directed review (scrub only the flagged spans) is trustworthy — reducing review burden by an order of magnitude versus frame-by-frame inspection without licensing missed failures.

## A4. Work performed (systematic investigation to date, August 2026)

**Design framing (August 1 2026).** The still-image inpainting capability (live scribble-infill loop, dual fast/quality LCM deployment, two-axis candidate versioning) was assessed for extension to the frame-accurate video annotator. Candidate video workflows were mapped against the existing architecture: (1) SAM 2 mask propagation — highest leverage, converts existing single-frame mask annotation into mask tracks, correction loop mirrors the proven live-inpaint UX shape; (2) mask-track-driven object removal — direct video analog of `remove`, composes with (1) into an "annotate once, edit the whole clip" workflow; (3) keyframe edit + flow propagation — reuses the deployed `nexus8-inpaint` Modal investment for generative video edits without per-frame diffusion; (4) point tracking for vector annotations (motion-aware interpolation of polylines/points); (5) automatic mask generation assists (video matting, text-prompted detection + propagation). Sequencing decision: (1) → (2) → (3), with (4)/(5) deferred. Practical constraints identified: video models consume frame sequences, not containers, so ingest must extract and stage frames server-side (ffprobe pipeline extension — note the dev media path already serves Range requests via `media_serve.py`, so browser playback is not the constraint; GPU-side frame access is); video jobs are batch-scale, requiring an async job + progress UI rather than the still pipeline's live-loop pattern.

**Licensing & model landscape survey (August 1 2026) — resolves U7.** A systematic survey of video inpainting / object removal models was conducted with every license claim verified against the actual repository LICENSE files or HuggingFace license tags (not secondary sources). Findings F1–F6 below. This survey materially changed the technical plan: the initial design assumed removal would require either licensing ProPainter or building a DIY flow-based fill; the survey established that purpose-built permissively-licensed alternatives now exist.

**UI/UX design (August 1 2026).** The video masking interaction model was designed against the shipped annotator surface (`AnnotatorPage.tsx`, `MaskLayersPanel.tsx`, `MaskLayerDetailPanel.tsx`, `RenderHistoryPanel.tsx`, `VideoTransport.tsx`), with the governing principle that video masking is the existing layer model *given a temporal dimension* rather than a new editing surface. Decisions:

- *Layers become tracks.* Mask mode — currently disabled for video (`isMaskCapable` in `AnnotationViewport.tsx` excludes video adapters) — is enabled; a mask layer on a video asset is a mask track. The layers panel carries over unchanged; its two visibility toggles extend temporally (Eye = mask overlay during playback, Image = removal/edit result during playback).
- *Track timeline.* The one genuinely new component: per-layer lanes under the transport scrubber showing ◆ keyframes (user-authored prompt/correction/edit frames, click-to-jump), propagated spans, and low-confidence spans (H7) directing review; propagation job progress renders into the lane as frames complete. Composes with the existing per-frame annotation visibility in `timeline.ts`.
- *Correction loop framed as propagate → review → correct → re-propagate.* Because propagation is a batch job (seconds to a minute), no attempt is made to mimic the still pipeline's live-loop feel; "Propagate" replaces the per-layer Generate action, runs non-blocking with a progress chip (frames done / total) and cancel, and a correction (editing the mask on any frame, which flips that frame to a keyframe) triggers span-limited re-propagation only.
- *Wholesale reuse.* The layer detail panel keeps its Parameters/History structure — video ops are a subset (`remove`, later `edit`), `gen_mode` maps to the removal tiers (fast = VACE-1.3B @ 480p preview, quality = VOID), prompt/negative-prompt remain (VACE removal is prompted). Render history entries become per-run filmstrips (4–5 sampled frames) with unchanged pin / apply-recipe / restore semantics via the two-axis versioning; the multi-variant strip likewise shows filmstrip thumbnails. The Phase 3 keyframe generative edit reuses the *complete* still-image mask/scribble editor (live LCM loop included) on the chosen frame; only the propagation-span visualization is new.
- *Result review is A/B comparison.* The core artist question for removal is "did it work, everywhere?"; a hold-to-compare control (hold shows original, release shows result) during playback exploits flicker-comparison for spotting temporal artifacts, and the existing dual-deck video adapter already provides the two synchronized video elements this requires.
- *Deferred by design:* per-frame vector editing of propagated masks (raster corrections via brush are the input), onion-skinning, simultaneous multi-object propagation UI, timeline zoom beyond a simple horizontal scale.

**Phase 0/1 implementation and first real-clip propagation (August 2–3 2026).** The frame staging pipeline (Phase 0), the SAM 2.1 Modal app (`nexus8-videoseg`, A10G, `sam2.1-hiera-small`, video-predictor API), the Django dispatch/poll/cancel endpoints (Phase 1.3), and the SPA track timeline with per-layer lanes, keyframe diamonds, propagated/low-confidence spans, user-selected in/out propagation span, and per-frame tinted mask overlay (Phases 1.4a–c, e) were implemented and debugged end to end against a real production-length clip (12 min, 17,620 frames, 1280×534). The first successful annotate → propagate → per-frame-overlay round trip completed August 2; the debugging arc itself produced findings F7–F11 below. Timeline state (keyframes, spans) rehydrates from persisted tracks on reload; drawn-but-unpropagated keyframes derive from the persisted CRDT strokes; the in/out span persists per-asset client-side.

**Experimental findings (August 2026).**

- **F1 — the incumbent removal models are confirmed non-commercial; no relicensing has occurred.** ProPainter remains under S-Lab License 1.0 (non-commercial; verified at https://github.com/sczhou/ProPainter/blob/main/LICENSE); E2FGVI remains CC BY-NC 4.0 (https://github.com/MCG-NKU/E2FGVI/blob/master/LICENSE). MiniMax-Remover (2025) — currently the best-reputed dedicated remover, a 6-step distillation of Wan2.1-1.3B — has **no code license** (repo has no LICENSE file; all rights reserved by default) and CC BY-NC 4.0 weights (https://huggingface.co/zibojia/minimax-remover). VideoPainter (TencentARC) is explicitly academic-only; RGVI is CC BY-NC and embeds S-Lab-licensed ProPainter components; FuseFormer and Deep Video Prior have no license grant. All are excluded from the commercial path.
- **F2 — Netflix VOID (2026) is a purpose-built, Apache-licensed removal model and the leading quality-tier candidate.** Code and weights both Apache 2.0 (https://github.com/netflix/void-model; HF weights tagged apache-2.0). Diffusion-based, mask-conditioned object removal *including removal of the object's physical effects* (shadows, reflections, interactions) — a capability no flow-completion model has. Input is a "quadmask" (remove / overlap / affected-interaction / preserve regions) that maps directly onto SAM 2 mask tracks. Two-pass inference: quadmask-conditioned inpainting over 85-frame windows (50 steps), then optical-flow warped-noise refinement for longer sequences. 384×672 default, up to 197 frames, 40 GB+ VRAM (A100-class — available on Modal). **Diligence flag:** VOID is fine-tuned from CogVideoX-Fun-V1.5-5b-InP; the upstream CogVideoX-5B weights license requires commercial registration with Zhipu and carries a 1M-visits/month cap (https://huggingface.co/THUDM/CogVideoX-5b/raw/main/LICENSE). Netflix distributes the derived weights as Apache 2.0; whether that extinguishes upstream conditions is a legal-diligence question, not resolvable technically. Noted for legal review if commercialization proceeds; not a blocker for experimentation.
- **F3 — Wan-VACE is the cleanest license chain and the designated prototype tier.** Alibaba's Wan2.1-VACE (1.3B and 14B) and Wan2.2-VACE-Fun-A14B are Apache 2.0 end to end — code (https://github.com/ali-vilab/VACE, https://github.com/Wan-Video/Wan2.1) and weights (HF apache-2.0 tags on Wan-AI/Wan2.1-VACE-14B and alibaba-pai/Wan2.2-VACE-Fun-A14B) — with no upstream ambiguity and the best ecosystem support (diffusers, ComfyUI, hosted endpoints). VACE supports masked video-to-video editing (feed video + binary mask track, generate fill), but is a *general text-guided editor*, not a dedicated remover: removal requires prompting for background and risks hallucinated fill (U4a). The 1.3B variant runs at 480p on ~8–24 GB VRAM — the cheapest per-job option, designated the prototype/preview tier (H4). Notably, MiniMax-Remover being a distillation of Wan2.1-1.3B for removal indicates the base model's capability is sufficient; the distillation (not the capability) is what carries the NC license.
- **F4 — DiffuEraser is Apache-licensed but its default pipeline re-imports the ProPainter license.** DiffuEraser (Alibaba/Taobao 2025; code https://github.com/lixiaowen-xw/DiffuEraser Apache 2.0, weights apache-2.0 on HF) is purpose-built for removal with better content completeness than ProPainter — but uses ProPainter as its prior-generation stage, and its README requires compliance with ProPainter's (non-commercial) license unless the prior is replaced. Commercial use therefore requires swapping the prior for an MIT-licensed flow stage (FGT/FGVC) — a real integration task. Held as a fallback rather than a primary candidate.
- **F5 — a fully-MIT classical tier exists but sits below ProPainter quality.** FGT (ECCV 2022, flow-guided transformer — closest classical analog to ProPainter), FGVC (ECCV 2020), and STTN (ECCV 2020) are all verified MIT. Cheap (<12 GB VRAM) and temporally stable by construction (they borrow real pixels via flow); viable for static-background / small-occlusion shots (H4's cheap tier) and as the DiffuEraser prior replacement (F4). Cannot synthesize never-visible content.
- **F6b — the track timeline requires a zoom/pan mechanism for long clips; proportional-position rendering is unworkable at production clip lengths.** Discovered during Phase 1.4 implementation (August 2026). The track timeline renders keyframe diamonds and segment bars at positions proportional to frame index / total frames. On production-length clips (2–10 minutes at 24 fps = ~3,000–14,400 frames), correction keyframes placed seconds apart occupy <0.1% of timeline width — on a 600 px lane, two prompt frames 1 s apart are ~1 px apart and their 16 px diamond markers fully overlap. This is not a rendering bug; it is a fundamental scale mismatch. The still-image inpainting workflow had no temporal axis and therefore no precedent for this constraint. **Required work (deferred, not in Phase 1–3 scope):** a zoomable/pannable timeline ruler — either a fixed-width zoomed view centred on the current keyframe cluster (analogous to NLE software "zoom to selection"), or a two-level overview + detail strip. Until implemented, artists on long clips must rely on the VideoTransport scrubber for frame navigation and accept that closely-spaced keyframe diamonds visually merge on the timeline overview. The architecture (CSS percentage-based lane rendering) is designed to support a zoom transform without requiring data-model changes — a `zoomRange: { startFrame, endFrame }` prop on `MaskTrackTimeline` is the anticipated interface.

- **F6 — the surrounding pipeline has clean permissive options throughout; known substitutions recorded.** SAM 2: Apache 2.0 (verified; https://github.com/facebookresearch/sam2) — no license obstacle for the propagation core; Meta ships no removal companion (their guidance is to pair SAM 2 with third-party generators). Point tracking: CoTracker3 is CC BY-NC (excluded); **TAPIR** (DeepMind) is Apache 2.0 and is the designated permissive substitute. Optical flow: RAFT is BSD. Robust Video Matting is GPL-3.0 — commercial-compatible but copyleft; acceptable as an isolated Modal service, flagged if ever embedded. Conditionally-usable base models recorded for completeness: LTX-Video weights are free including commercial use *below* US$10 M annual revenue (paid license above; https://huggingface.co/Lightricks/LTX-Video — relevant if the LTX-based EraserDiT ever releases code); HunyuanVideo's community license is void in the EU/UK/South Korea including use of outputs, and requires a separate grant above 100 M MAU — excluded on territorial grounds alone.

- **F7 — frame-transport economics resolved (U3a): span-scoped JPEG staging at preview resolution, not full-clip PNG.** Measured on the 12-min test clip (17,620 frames, 1280×534): full-clip native PNG extraction produced **10 GB** of frames in ~5 min — and the resulting archive cannot be shipped as a Modal function argument at all (a 140 MB PNG archive for a 324-frame span stalled the synchronous `.spawn()` upload for minutes). Three compounding reductions were required: (a) **span-scoped extraction** — ffmpeg `select=between(n,start,end)` with a `-frames:v` bound is frame-accurate (verified by byte-identical comparison against full-clip extraction) and stops decoding at the span end; (b) the **480p preview tier** for segmentation (masks are upscaled to native at display); (c) **JPEG staging instead of PNG** — SAM 2's video predictor natively consumes JPEG frame sequences, so the original PNG pipeline was being decoded and re-encoded to JPEG inside the GPU container anyway; lossless transport bought nothing. Net result: a 324-frame span stages in **0.8 s**, archives to **14.1 MB** (vs. 140 MB PNG — 10×), and dispatches in ~5 s. Spans re-stage from cache per (asset, tier, span) key. The full-clip / native tier remains available for a final-quality pass. Larger payloads (long spans, native tier) should move to a Modal Volume rather than function-argument transport — recorded as the next transport step, not yet needed at experimental scale.
- **F8 — full-clip propagation is not viable on production-length clips; span-scoping is a correctness requirement, not an optimization.** SAM 2's video predictor loads every frame's features into GPU memory at `init_state`; a 17,620-frame clip would OOM an A10G (and any single GPU) regardless of transport. The interaction model was therefore changed from "propagate the clip" to "propagate a user-selected in/out span" (timeline Set In / Set Out, persisted per asset), with a server-side hard cap (600 frames/job) and prompts outside the span dropped server-side. SAM 2 propagation is forward-only from a prompt frame, so span coverage requires a keyframe at or near the span start — a UX constraint documented for the correction loop (1.4d). This bounds H1's "propagation horizon" question: on long clips the horizon is memory-bounded per job before it is drift-bounded; drift (H1 proper) is measured *within* spans.
- **F9 — coordinate provenance through a multi-resolution prompt chain fails silently as plausible-but-wrong segmentation.** First real-clip propagation confidently segmented a background tree instead of the prompted foreground character. Two compounding coordinate-space defects: (a) annotator stroke geometry is stored **frame-local** (an anchor frame at pointer-down plus offset points beginning at (0,0)) — used raw, every prompt collapsed toward the image's top-left corner; (b) prompt clicks were expressed in native pixels (1280×534) while the staged frames SAM 2 receives are 854×356 (F7's preview tier) — a ~1.5× displacement even after (a) was fixed. The failure mode is the significant part: **no error is produced; the model returns a high-confidence mask of the wrong object**, indistinguishable at the API level from success. Resolution: an explicit conversion contract (frame-local → world/native px via the annotator's canonical `framePointToWorld` → staged px, rescaled server-side against the *measured* staged frame dimensions with the client-declared `source_size`), plus a permanent server-side log of final click coordinates as a diagnostic. Directly analogous failure classes await Phase 2 (mask tracks fed to removal models at yet another resolution) — the contract generalizes.
- **F10 — click-prompt fidelity ceiling identified on first real-clip inspection (H1/H7 evidence).** The delivered mask is correct but boundary-imprecise on a subset of frames. Attributed causes, in measured order of impact: (a) segmentation at 854×356 upscaled 1.5× to native — a ±1 px model error becomes ±2–3 px on screen, a systematic softness accepted in the F7 payload trade; (b) **prompt information loss** — the artist's painted mask is reduced to ≤3 sampled click points, discarding the boundary information already provided; SAM 2's `add_new_mask` accepts full mask prompts and is the identified highest-value remedy (rasterize the drawn stroke, prompt with it directly); (c) **propagation drift between keyframes** — the first real track showed a continuous sub-0.7-confidence run spanning most of the inter-keyframe interval (frames ~637–700 between keyframes at 621 and 669/701), consistent with H7's premise that confidence flags the spans a human would correct; (d) the deployed variant is `sam2.1-hiera-small` (iteration-speed choice); base-plus/large improve boundary quality at higher GPU cost. Remedy ladder recorded: mask prompts → correction keyframes (1.4d) → larger variant → higher staging tier.
- **F11 — CRDT layer ids are not durable identities; mask tracks must be the anchor, with the version pointer on symlinks (H2/U2 evidence).** Layer ids in the Yjs annotation doc proved unstable across sessions (mask layers are auto-created with fresh UUIDs; a layer deleted-and-recreated orphans everything keyed to its id). A track keyed by (video, layer_id) therefore silently detached from its layer on reload — masks, keyframes and spans all vanished from the UI while remaining intact in the DB. Resolution inverts the dependency: **the track list is the durable source of truth** — a per-video track index endpoint lets the client restore lost layers *by their original id* (re-inserting them into the CRDT doc, which then persists them, self-healing the identity), with the layer's name/colour persisted onto the track relation at propagation time so restoration is faithful. Two H2 data-model details confirmed in implementation: the entity model has **no current-version column** — the canonical version pointer is the `latest` symlink (audited via symlink events); and per-frame mask rasters are currently stored base64-inline in `Version.data` JSONB, which round-trips correctly at experiment scale but should migrate to the content-addressed store (per the original H2 formulation) before multi-track or long-span use.
- **F12 — larger model + negative clicks are the two implemented responses to the F9/F10 prompt-fidelity findings; mask prompts remain the open lever.** Two remedies from the F10 ladder were built and are in the pipeline. (a) **Model tier:** the deployed variant was moved `sam2.1-hiera-small` → `sam2.1-hiera-large` (~46M → ~224M params, the top SAM 2.1 tier) on the same A10G; `init_state(offload_video_to_cpu=True)` keeps the larger encoder plus a 600-frame span within 24 GB without an A100 (`offload_state_to_cpu` deliberately left off — its per-frame CPU round-trip is only warranted for videos far longer than the 600-frame cap). (b) **Negative clicks (SAM label 0):** the click-prompt vocabulary was positive-only, which structurally cannot correct the F9 wrong-object failure (a positive click on the character can't say "not the tree"). Negative prompting was added as a ⌥/Alt-held brush stroke (stateless modifier, matching NLE/Photoshop subtract-from-selection; chosen over a persistent toggle mode specifically to eliminate mode-confusion errors during precise work), rendered red with a live red cursor ring, persisted on the stroke geometry so it survives reload, and emitted as `positive:false` through the same ≤3-point sampling and F9 coordinate-rescaling path. The Modal predictor already consumed label-0 points — the gap was purely that the client never produced them. **Design constraint noted:** with the current brush active, ⌥/Alt is reassigned from video-pan to negative-stroke (pan remains on middle-mouse / select tool), and the feature is gated to video mask mode so the still-image rasterize-mask pipeline is unaffected. **Still open (highest-value):** `add_new_mask` mask prompts (F10b) — negative clicks disambiguate *which* object; they do not recover the boundary fidelity discarded by click-sampling. The ordering of expected impact remains mask prompts > larger model ≈ negative clicks > staging tier, to be confirmed against the H1/1.5 experiments.
- **F13 — mask prompts (`add_new_mask`) delivered; the artist's painted region now conditions SAM 2 directly, closing the F10b lever.** The remaining high-value remedy from F10/F12 is implemented end to end. A prompt frame carrying any positive paint is now sent as a **mask prompt** rather than sampled clicks: the painted strokes are rasterized to a binary PNG at native resolution (reusing the still-image `rasterizeMask` path, honouring brush radius), Modal resizes it to the staged frame size (nearest-neighbour, kept binary) and calls `add_new_mask(obj_id=1, mask)`. This recovers exactly the boundary information the ≤3-click sampling discarded (F10b), so painting fidelity now translates to mask fidelity — a rough fill still helps (SAM refines from it), a tight fill approaches exact. **Negative integration — the non-obvious design point:** SAM 2 makes mask-prompts and point-prompts *mutually exclusive per (frame, object)*, so positive-mask + negative-click on the same frame is not expressible. Rather than drop the F12 negatives, they are **carved out of the mask client-side** — positives painted white, negatives erased via canvas `destination-out`, and Modal reads the resulting **alpha channel** as the object region (paint minus negatives). This preserves both signals in the one channel SAM accepts. Graceful fallback retained: a frame with *only* negative strokes (a correction adding no positive region) still emits negative **click** prompts, so the correction path keeps working. Backend is transparent to prompt type (mask frames carry no `clicks`, so the F9 click-rescale loop skips them and `mask_b64` rides the span-filter/rebase untouched); Modal logs `Adding MASK prompt at frame N: <px> of <w>x<h>`. **Now delivered from the F10 remedy ladder:** mask prompts (F13) + larger model + negative clicks (F12); **still open:** correction keyframes (1.4d, span-limited re-propagation) and higher staging tier. The prompt-fidelity thread F9 → F10 → F12 → F13 is closed pending the H1/1.5 quality measurement.
  - **F13a — points vs. mask is an artist-selectable prompt mode, not a forced default; the two carry genuinely different SAM 2 semantics.** The first F13 implementation *forced* mask prompts whenever a frame had positive paint, which silently removed the cheap "one dab, SAM infers the whole silhouette" workflow. This surfaced a real behavioural distinction worth recording as an experimental parameter, not just a UI preference: a **click** lets SAM infer the object's full *extent* from a point (it grows well beyond the click), whereas a **mask** anchors SAM to the painted region (it stays close to what was drawn). They are different conditioning signals with different failure modes — clicks under-specify extent (may grab a part or the wrong scale), masks over-constrain if the paint is sloppy. Resolution: a per-propagation **Prompt: Points / Mask** toggle (mask-panel header, video only), defaulting to **Points** to preserve the low-effort feel. Points mode routes every stroke — including erase-carved negatives — through the click-sampling path; Mask mode rasterizes. This makes the points-vs-mask trade a *measurable* variable for the H1/1.5 experiments (which prompt kind converges faster / needs fewer corrections, as a function of object complexity), rather than a baked-in assumption.
- **F14 — the correction loop is span-limited re-propagation + prior-version merge, and the merge is the mechanism that makes H1's "corrections-to-converge" measurable.** Phase 1.4d delivered. The insight that made it cheap: a correction is not a new concept but *another propagation over a smaller span* through the already-built span-scoped pipeline — editing frame *k* and hitting **Correct** re-propagates only `[earliest edit this pass, existing span end]`, and the backend overlays those re-run frames onto the prior version's frames by index (frames before the edit keep their masks *and* provenance; `prior_version_id` + `corrected_frames` recorded on the new version, extending H2's always-new-version convention with correction lineage). Two design problems surfaced and were resolved: (a) **the sub-span re-runs statelessly** (each Modal call re-inits), so the correction frame must re-establish the object — a pure erase relative to an invisible prior mask would have nothing to segment from; resolved by **compositing the prior mask + the delta client-side** (fetch the prior frame mask from the serve endpoint, union positive strokes, `destination-out` the negatives) so even a small erase-only correction carries a complete object mask into the re-run. (b) **downstream anchors**: keyframes that fall inside the correction range are re-sent as prompts, so a good frame further along still anchors the re-propagation rather than being overwritten by drift from *k*. Forward-only in this MVP (a correction fixes *k* → end, not backward from where drift was *noticed* to where it *began*); bidirectional (`propagate reverse=True` to the previous keyframe) and next-keyframe range-capping are recorded as the next refinements. Experimental consequence: each Correct is now a **discrete, logged, bounded event** with its `corrected_frames` recorded, so H1's convergence metric ("corrections per shot to reach production-usable") and the F8 span-vs-drift distinction become directly countable from the version history rather than reconstructed.
  - **F14a — a silent state bug: negative strokes lost their polarity on any dragged commit, so erase/subtract only ever worked as zero-motion dabs.** Surfaced while adding a live erase preview. The brush draft-update and commit paths reconstructed the geometry as `{kind, points, radius}`, **dropping the `negative` flag** the moment a stroke had more than one point — so every *dragged* negative stroke committed as a *positive* prompt (its red colour survived via `style`, masking the bug visually). This means the F12 negative-click / F13 mask-erase features were effectively inert for real strokes until now; both the live preview and the persisted prompt data were affected. Fix: preserve `negative` through both reconstructions. **Root cause found on a second pass:** fixing the two viewport reconstructions restored the *live draft* preview but committed strokes *still* lost the flag — because the canonical store path, `normalizeGeometry` in the annotation schema, rebuilds every geometry to a whitelist of known fields on write and silently dropped `negative` there too. The draft is in-memory (kept the flag → preview worked); the moment it was committed through the CRDT store it was normalized away (→ red on mouse-up, and bucketed as *positive* by the prompt builder). The durable lesson: a new semantic field on a serialized type has to be added at the **schema/normalization boundary**, not just the interactive reconstruction sites — the schema is the one that decides what persists. (Corollary: strokes drawn before the schema fix are already stored flag-less and don't retroactively become negative — they must be redrawn.)
  - **F14b — erase is shown as the mask receding, not a red overlay (direct-manipulation feedback).** Drawing an erase as a red stroke *on top of* the mask read as "adding red", the opposite of the intent. The overlay now punches the erase region out of the just-drawn mask (`destination-out`), so an ⌥/Alt drag reads as the mask melting away to reveal the frame — while still showing red on frames with *no* mask (a "not this" hint has nothing to subtract from, so the stroke itself is the only available marker). The red cursor ring remains the mode affordance. Small but load-bearing: without live subtractive feedback the artist can't tell whether an erase "took" until after a re-propagation.
- **F15 — mask-prompt erases are a weak exclusion signal; fine removal edits need negative *clicks*, and fine detail needs staging resolution — two complementary controls added.** Observed while trimming mask bleed around a character's hands: small erases composited into a correction mask (F14) frequently failed to "stick." Two compounding causes, matching the F10/F9 resolution lessons: (a) **`add_new_mask` is a soft hint, not a hard constraint** — SAM re-segments from image features and *heals over* a small carve-out, especially near an object's own ambiguous boundary (fingers); and (b) **resolution** — a native-res trim of a few pixels is washed out through the native → 480p staging → SAM's low-res internal mask-prompt encoding. A negative *point* ("this pixel is NOT the object") is a categorically stronger exclusion signal than a hole in a soft mask. Two responses shipped: (1) **correction routing by intent** — a correction whose edit *removes* (has negative strokes) is now sent as **clicks**: negative points on the erase strokes plus positive seed points sampled from the prior mask's interior (`getImageData` grid-sample, scaled to native) so the object stays established; a purely *additive* correction still composites to a mask (where a mask's boundary fidelity helps). SAM's mask/point mutual-exclusivity per frame forces this either/or, so the routing picks the stronger tool for the edit's *direction*. (2) **staging-resolution control** — a `preview_480p / preview_720p / native` selector (mask panel, video only) threaded to `propagation_params.staging_tier`; higher tiers preserve thin trims at higher upload/GPU cost, per the F7 payload economics. Both are also H1/1.5 experimental variables (does intent-routed clicking reduce corrections-to-converge for removal-type edits? at which resolution do fine trims survive?), instrumented rather than assumed. Open: mask-prompt + negative-point on the *same* frame is impossible in SAM 2, so a correction still can't simultaneously assert a precise positive boundary *and* strong negative exclusion — a genuine model constraint, not an implementation gap.
- **F16 — first real-shot correction convergence data (H1): negative clicks overcorrect at *touching-object* boundaries, and the mitigations are (a) balance positives with negatives, (b) a prior-mask seed that excludes the erased region, (c) a deterministic erode alternative for uniform bleed.** Test case: a character's hand resting on a railing, mask bleeding from hand onto railing. Findings from iterating the F14/F15 correction loop on it: (1) **Seed contamination bug + fix.** The positive seed sampled from the prior mask to keep the object established was sampled from the mask *including* the bleed — so it planted positive points on the very railing the negatives were removing, directly cancelling them. Fixed by filtering seed points that fall inside any erase stroke (distance-to-negative-stroke test in native px). This is a general hazard of "seed positives from the current mask" whenever the current mask is what you're correcting. (2) **Overcorrection is intrinsic at contact boundaries, not user error.** A negative click near an ambiguous hand↔railing contact makes SAM retreat *past* the boundary into the object — because "not railing" and "keep hand" occupy the same region in the model's view. Observed convergence was >2 corrections/frame (H1's ≤3 target sits at the hard end for touching objects). (3) **The technique that most reduces passes: pair each negative with a nearby positive** on the object side of the contact — the negative alone leaves the object edge unanchored and SAM over-retreats; a positive dab pins it. This is now the recommended workflow, and it is measurable H1 signal (convergence *with* vs *without* positive-anchoring). (4) **Diagnosis for the tool gap:** for a *uniform* mask halo (over-inclusion everywhere), clicks are the wrong instrument entirely — a deterministic **erode/dilate (mask tightness)** post-process trims the boundary predictably without SAM re-segmentation, so it can't overcorrect the way clicks do; for a *localized* leak, clicks with positive-anchoring remain correct. Erode/dilate is therefore the next control to build (it was surveyed in 1.4f's deferred list; this shot promoted it from "nice-to-have" to "the right tool for bleed"). Net H1 read: the correction loop *works*, but convergence is boundary-dependent — clean silhouettes converge in 1–2, touching/occluding-contact boundaries need more and benefit from a non-SAM deterministic trim, which is itself a fundable experimental result about where interactive-SAM correction hits diminishing returns.
- **F17 — the perceived-quality gap vs. Meta's SAM 2 demo is an interaction-architecture gap, not a model gap; closed with an interactive prompt-frame preview loop that "compiles" clicks into a mask prompt.** Comparative analysis (same weights on both sides — hiera-large locally vs. the demo): the demo's superior single-click results come from (a) **closed-loop prompting** — every click runs just the mask decoder against a live GPU session and shows *that frame's* mask in ~100 ms, so the human iterates 3–4 cheap corrections *before* committing to propagation, whereas our pipeline was **open-loop** (draw blind → ~80 s propagation → first feedback); (b) **persistent session state** (the demo holds `init_state` per user session; we re-stage/re-init per job — the statelessness that forced the F14 prior-mask gymnastics); (c) **progressive result streaming** vs. our block-until-done; (d) lower fixed resolution. Response implemented: an **interactive preview loop** — a `preview(frame_jpeg, clicks)` method on the existing warm Modal class using SAM 2's *image* predictor built from the same Apache-2.0 checkpoint (embedding cached by frame hash, so repeat clicks on a frame skip the encoder); a Django single-frame endpoint (frame staged/cached individually, clicks rescaled per the F9 contract); and a debounced client loop — each committed dab/erase refreshes a per-(layer, frame) preview mask overlaid in place of the track mask, with SAM's confidence shown. The architectural resolution worth recording: **clicks are the interaction, the mask is the contract** — when the artist propagates, the *settled preview mask* is sent as the frame's `add_new_mask` prompt ("compile" step). This dissolves the F15/F13 points-vs-mask trade for refined frames: click semantics during interactive refinement, exact-boundary mask conditioning for propagation, sidestepping SAM's mask/point per-frame exclusivity by separating the two in *time* rather than choosing one. Displaying the preview also intentionally shows SAM's *disagreement* with an erase (the F14b hole-punch is suppressed under a preview) — the artist sees what the model actually accepted, pre-propagation. License note: original implementation against the Apache-2.0 `sam2` package and Apache-2.0 SAM 2.1 weights (F6); **no code from Meta's demo app was copied** — only the session/preview architecture pattern was studied. Remaining gap vs. demo, deferred: progressive per-frame result streaming during propagation, and a true persistent video session (would eliminate correction re-init entirely — the natural Phase-1 endgame if preview-loop uptake proves it out).

- **F18 — the F17 conditioning gap is closed: the interactive loop now implements the demo's two remaining mechanisms — logits chaining between clicks, and a persistent GPU propagation session so corrections refine the *propagated* mask (August 5 2026).** Source analysis of `sam2` established that the demo's remaining edge over our pipeline was two forms of *mask-prior conditioning* we discarded. (a) **Click-to-click chaining (stateless preview).** The official SAM interactive protocol is: first click `multimask_output=True` (best-of-3); every subsequent click single-mask **plus the previous prediction's 256×256 low-res logits as `mask_input`**. The F17 preview implemented only the multimask half, so each refinement click re-solved from bare coordinates — the hardest form of the problem, and why added clicks sometimes made results *worse*. The preview now caches the winning low-res logits alongside the F17 embedding cache and chains them, guarded by a prefix-extension check on the click list (an undo must not chain against a mask that included the removed click; it falls back to a fresh solve). Measured on the deployed app: second click 39 ms vs 599 ms cold, score 0.989 → 0.995. (b) **Session-conditioned corrections (F17's deferred "persistent video session" endgame).** After a propagation the Modal container now *retains* the video-predictor inference state (keyed `asset:layer`, LRU-capped at 2 — ≈1 GB of per-frame memory features per 600-frame hiera-large span; `max_containers=1` pins clicks to the container holding the state). A `session_click` method runs `add_new_points_or_box` against that state, and SAM 2's own lookup feeds the frame's existing output back as `prev_sam_mask_logits` — so a correction click *edits the propagated mask* rather than re-solving, which is precisely the demo's correction mechanism. The preview endpoint routes to the session when the client reports one (span+tier validated against the session's stored meta; a mismatch or a recycled container degrades gracefully to the stateless chained preview). Verified end to end: a **negative-only** click on a distractor in a propagated frame returned the object mask intact (100% of baseline area, score 1.0, 267 ms) — impossible without conditioning, since a lone negative click carries no object signal. Three architectural consequences: (1) **negative-only corrections are first-class inside a propagated span** — the prior mask establishes the object, so bleed is trimmed with erase strokes alone (previously such frames produced no preview at all); (2) the F15 removal-correction routing (interior-seeded positives + negative clicks) is **demoted from primary mechanism to fallback** — its seed-point gymnastics compensated for statelessness (F14's "re-establish the object" problem), and the F16 seed-contamination hazard vanishes with it whenever a session is live; (3) propagation deliberately **rebuilds the state from the full prompt set every run** rather than propagating incrementally — the session exists for click conditioning *between* runs, while each run's authority derives only from current prompts, so deleted strokes cannot leave stale conditioning behind, and the F17 compile step (settled preview → `add_new_mask` prompt) guarantees the propagated result matches what the artist approved on screen. Accepted costs at experimental scale: `max_containers=1` serializes all GPU work (a click queues behind a running propagation), and a session lost to container scale-down silently downgrades correction previews to stateless until the next propagation. One test-design lesson: on a *featureless* synthetic object, a negative click **on** the object suppresses the entire mask — there is no boundary evidence to trim along — so synthetic fixtures can evaluate *conditioning* but not *trim quality*; the latter needs real-content texture.

- **F19 — part-level selectivity ("just the hair") is decided at gesture-sampling time, before the model ever runs: SAM's granularity ladder is only reachable through a single-point prompt, and stroke→multi-point sampling was silently discarding that (August 5 2026).** Comparative observation against the Meta demo: demo clicks are *selective* — a click grabs the hair, the face, the hands — while our equivalent gesture grabbed the whole person. Root cause is an interaction-vocabulary difference, not a model or protocol one. SAM's subpart/part/whole granularity lives in its three multimask output tokens, and those candidates are generated **only for an ambiguous prompt — in practice, exactly one point** (the video predictor hard-codes `multimask_max_pt_num=1`; our F17/F18 preview gates multimask on `len(clicks)==1`). The demo's vocabulary is one precise point per gesture, so the first click always gets the granularity ladder, and every later click is prior-anchored (F18) so refinement *stays* at the chosen granularity. Our vocabulary is brush strokes sampled to ≤3 points spread along the path (F10) — so **any dragged stroke produced ≥2 dispersed positives, which (a) disabled multimask and (b) semantically asked the single-mask head for "the object spanning all of these points"**. Points straddling part boundaries make the minimal consistent answer the whole object; the model was answering the question it was asked. (The F15 interior-seeded positives compounded the same bias in corrections; and pre-F18, the absence of prior anchoring let granularity drift upward as clicks accumulated.) This is F10's sampling finding surfacing a second time: ≤3-point sampling discards not just *boundary* information but *granularity intent*. **Fix — gesture semantics in `sampleStrokeClicks`, one site covering both the preview loop and prompt compilation:** a **dab** (stroke extent within the brush's own radius) now emits exactly **one point at its centroid**, restoring single-point multimask part selection; a real **drag** keeps the ≤3-point "cover this region" semantics. The resulting vocabulary is demo-consistent and self-revealing: dab = select at part granularity, dab again = prior-anchored additive refinement (F18), drag = span the object, ⌥-dab = point exclusion. Not adopted, recorded as options if part selection proves insufficient: a dedicated point-click tool separate from the brush (explicit rather than inferred semantics), and candidate selection by smallest-containing-mask instead of `argmax(score)` when scores are close (our argmax matches the demo, so no parity change was warranted).

- **F20 — H2's JSONB-inline raster convention measured at real scale: per-clip storage is tolerable, but the F14 correction merge copies the whole span into every version and the serve path parses the full version blob per frame; resolved by a FileSet overlay design (immutable file sequences with discontinuous ranges) — see [[FILESET_MODEL.md]] (August 6 2026).** Measurement on the dev DB (9 mask-track versions): ~1.7–2.8 KB/frame at 480p on disk (TOASTed JSONB), ~1.2 MB for a 600-frame version, ~7.8 MB total — the *size* half of F11's "round-trips correctly at experiment scale" holds. Three scaling behaviours do not. (a) **Correction runs materialize the entire span.** The F14 merge overlays re-run frames onto the prior version's frames *by copying all of them into the new version's JSON* — observed directly as four ~1 MB copies of the same 366-frame track from one correction session. N corrections = N full-span copies: H2's always-new-version convention, in the JSONB representation, carries a hidden O(span × corrections) storage cost that the correction-loop workflow (F14/F16: multiple corrections per shot is the *normal* case) multiplies. (b) **Read amplification on the hot path**: `get_mask_for_frame` deserializes the full ~1.2 MB version JSON to extract one ~2 KB mask, once per frame served to the overlay. (c) **`native`-tier masks (F15's resolution control) will be ~3–5× larger per frame**, compounding both. A track-lifecycle gap was closed in the same pass: layer deletion only removed the CRDT layer, so F11's restore-from-track-list resurrected deleted layers on every reload — a DELETE endpoint (unlink relation; optional `purge=1` destroying track + versions) now completes the lifecycle. **Resolution designed (not yet implemented): the FileSet primitive** — an immutable, sealed package of files (printf-padded pattern, canonical inclusive `[[start,end]]` pairs, discontinuous ranges native) referenced from entity versions through an ordered join table with last-wins per-frame resolution. A correction then stores *only its re-run frames* as a small discontinuous layer over the base FileSet — which answers U2(b) directly: a corrected track is **both** a new version *and* a patch over a frame span, the version being a cheap ordered list of layer references. Provenance becomes structural (the frame's owning FileSet identifies its run), the F14 Python merge loop is deleted, serving becomes one file read, and GC is ref-counting on the join table. The same primitive is designed to absorb the Phase 0 staging cache (span reuse becomes range intersection rather than exact-directory match) and future image-sequence assets — recorded in [[FILESET_MODEL.md]] with the migration plan.

## A5. Technological advancement sought

- An **interactive mask-track correction loop** coupling a frame-accurate browser annotator to serverless-GPU video segmentation, with a re-propagation protocol (span-limited vs. full-clip) determined experimentally by convergence behaviour — no established precedent in production annotation tooling.
- A **provenance-complete representation of partially-human, partially-model-generated mask tracks** within a linear-versioned asset system, extending the two-axis versioning scheme developed for still-image candidates to per-frame-range video outputs.
- Experimental determination of the **quality/cost/steerability boundaries between removal model classes** (general masked video editor vs. purpose-built diffusion remover vs. classical flow completion) on production content, under the constraint of commercially viable licensing — including whether prompt-strategy findings from still-image inpainting transfer to video masked editing.
- A **keyframe-edit propagation technique** hybridizing optical-flow transfer with fast-diffusion disocclusion fill, with its viability envelope (frames-per-keyframe vs. motion magnitude) established experimentally.
- A **confidence-directed review interface** — empirical determination of whether model confidence (or cheap derived temporal signals) predicts human correction sites well enough to compress review of hundreds of propagated frames into a handful of flagged timeline spans, and the interaction design that makes that trustworthy.

  - **1.4f** ✅ Auto-masking controls exposed: **Prompt (Points/Mask)** kind (F13a; in Points mode a dab is one part-selective point, a drag spans the object — F19) and **Res (480p/720p/native)** staging tier (F15); display controls for the correction workflow — mask overlay opacity slider, **Result/Edit/Neg** prompt-display modes (solo-negatives isolation), per-frame **Clear −**, select-and-delete of individual strokes. Deferred knobs: **grow/shrink (erode/dilate)** — promoted to next-to-build by F16 (the right deterministic tool for uniform mask bleed, where clicks overcorrect); mask tightness (logit threshold), auto-stop-on-confidence, multi-object still surveyed-only.
  - **1.4g** ✅ Interactive prompt-frame preview loop (F17): dab/erase → debounced single-frame SAM preview (~sub-second warm) shown in place of the track mask, with confidence; the settled preview compiles into the propagation as that frame's mask prompt. Refined per F18: click-to-click logits chaining, and post-propagation clicks conditioned on the retained GPU session (`session_click`). Deferred: progressive propagation streaming.
  - **1.4e** ✅ Per-frame mask overlay during playback/scrub: per-frame RGBA masks fetched lazily, tinted with the layer colour via `source-in` compositing, cached per (layer, version, frame); redraws on the frame tick.
- **1.5** Experiments (H1, U1): on ≥5 real clips of varying motion/occlusion — single-prompt propagation span before unusable drift; corrections-to-converge per shot; full-clip vs. span-limited re-propagation latency and quality; behaviour past occlusion events. Log per clip.
- **1.6** Experiment (H7, U6): on the same clips, record every frame the artist corrects, then compute how well each candidate signal would have flagged it — raw SAM 2 per-frame confidence, frame-to-frame mask-area discontinuity, consecutive-frame IoU drop, distance from nearest prompt frame — as recall/precision at the span level. Adopt the best signal (or combination) for the timeline's low-confidence lane; if recall is poor across all signals, demote the lane from "review here only" to advisory and document the false-negative rationale (U6c).
- **Exit criteria:** scribble-on-one-frame → corrected, production-usable mask track on real clips through the timeline UI; H1 convergence numbers documented; H7 signal evaluation documented; track versioning round-trips through the entity model.

## Phase 2 — Object removal (M + N8, ~1–2 wk) — tests H4, H5, U4

- **2.1** Modal app `nexus8-videoremove`, tier 1: **Wan2.1-VACE-1.3B** at 480p (preview tier). Inputs = staged frames + mask track + prompt; removal steered by background-description prompting.
- **2.2** Prompt-strategy sweep (H5, U4a): background description vs. instruction phrasing vs. empty prompt + negative-prompt-only, on ≥3 shot classes (static background, moving camera, textured background). Record hallucination incidents (objects appearing in fill) per configuration; compare against INP-F8's still-image prompt model.
- **2.3** Tier 2: **VOID** on A100-40GB. Quadmask populated from the SAM 2 track (object region = track mask; interaction region empty in the first pass, then dilated-band variant). Test on- and off-distribution content; shots longer than the 85-frame window to exercise the second-pass consistency mechanism. Record per-job cost and wall-clock. (U4b)
- **2.4** Tier 0 comparison: **FGT** (MIT) on static-background shots — establish where the free classical tier suffices. (U4c)
- **2.5** Result storage: removal output ingested as a derived video asset version with full provenance (`VersionLink` to source video version + mask track version; model, prompt, parameters in `Version.data`), following the INP-F13 pattern.
- **2.6** SPA result review (per the A4 UI/UX design): result playback via the layer's Image toggle; hold-to-compare original/result during playback (dual-deck adapter provides the two synchronized video elements); render-history entries as per-run filmstrips (4–5 sampled frames) with existing pin/apply-recipe semantics; multi-variant strip with filmstrip thumbnails.
- **Exit criteria:** end-to-end annotate → propagate → remove on a real clip in both tiers; hallucination-rate and quality comparison VACE vs. VOID vs. FGT documented per shot class; per-job cost table.

## Phase 3 — Keyframe edit propagation (M + SPA, ~1–2 wk, contingent) — tests H6, U5

- **3.1** Keyframe edit: existing `nexus8-inpaint` scribble-infill on a chosen frame (reuses the deployed still pipeline unchanged).
- **3.2** Propagation: RAFT (BSD) flow chained from the keyframe; edited region warped forward/backward within the mask track; disocclusion detection via forward-backward flow consistency.
- **3.3** Disocclusion fill: LCM fast mode (INP-F6) per affected frame, constrained to the disoccluded sub-region, blended into the propagated edit.
- **3.4** Experiments (H6): frames-per-keyframe before perceptual degradation, as a function of motion magnitude (slow pan / handheld / fast subject motion); temporal-stability assessment of the hybrid vs. naive per-frame diffusion baseline.
- **3.5** Decision gate: if the viability envelope is too narrow (re-key interval ≈ 1–5 frames on ordinary content), abandon the hybrid and evaluate Wan-VACE reference-frame conditioning as the generative-edit path instead.
- **Exit criteria:** H6 envelope documented with clip evidence; go/no-go decision on the hybrid recorded.

## Cross-cutting

- **Risk — U1 (correction-loop divergence):** highest experimental risk; if span-limited re-propagation cannot keep per-correction latency interactive, the loop falls back to batch "propagate → review → correct → re-propagate" with no live quality — infrastructure unchanged.
- **Risk — U3 (payload/cost):** if frame transport dominates job time, evaluate staging frames directly to a Modal volume at ingest.
- **Risk — F2 diligence flag:** VOID's upstream license question must be answered before it ships in a commercial path; Wan-VACE (F3) is the unconditionally clean fallback for all tiers.
- **License rules adopted (from F1–F6):** ProPainter, E2FGVI, MiniMax-Remover, CoTracker3, VideoPainter, RGVI, FuseFormer excluded from all shipped paths; TAPIR substitutes CoTracker; DiffuEraser only with prior-stage replacement; RVM only as an isolated service.
- **Out of scope this experiment:** audio; multi-object simultaneous tracks; streaming/progressive preview of removal jobs; text-prompted auto-labeling (Grounding DINO + SAM 2); video depth workflows; multiple simultaneous users.
